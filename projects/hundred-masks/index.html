The hard parts, and what I did

Several ways to act. One readable turn.

Combine moving, merging and summoning while keeping each tactical consequence readable on a paper-theatre board.

  1. A painted stage that behaves like a board

    The problem
    The stage is hand-painted, but taps, highlights and where a character’s feet land all need exact squares. Separate maths for each drifts apart.
    What I did
    The squares are generated in SceneKit and fitted to the painting. Hit-testing, highlights and footing all read from that one projection; the art is only the material.
    Result
    A test drags onto every visible square and checks the hit lands on the same cell, on every phone size.

  2. Undo a whole turn, not a single move

    The problem
    One turn can include a summon, a move, a merge and the enemy’s reply. Undoing only the last step leaves coins, masks and hand out of sync.
    What I did
    The core records complete turn history, so undo restores the hand, masks and every piece’s position together.
    Result
    Covered by tests for cancellation, summon-then-move and reward de-duplication.
